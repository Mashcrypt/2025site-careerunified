What candidates need from a good job post

Share this post

Clear requirements, realistic expectations, and a visible application route help the right candidates understand the opportunity and respond with confidence.

Why clarity matters

A job post is often a candidate's first meaningful contact with an employer. When the role is vague, candidates have to guess what the work involves, whether they are qualified and how to apply. That uncertainty reduces trust and can discourage capable people from applying.

Unclear wording also attracts applications from people who do not understand the actual requirements. A clear post gives suitable candidates enough information to make an informed decision and helps recruiters spend less time sorting through mismatched applications.

Start with the role

Begin with the information candidates need to identify the opportunity quickly. Use a specific, searchable job title rather than an internal title or an inflated label. Name the department or team, location and employment type, such as permanent, fixed-term, part-time or internship.

State whether the role is office-based, remote or hybrid and explain any required office days. Where it helps candidates understand the position, include who the role reports to or where it sits in the team structure.

Explain the work

Describe the main responsibilities in plain language and identify the first priorities for the person joining. Candidates should be able to picture the work rather than read a list of broad phrases.

  • Name the tools, systems or methods used regularly.
  • Explain what progress or success could look like.
  • Describe the level of independence expected.
  • Distinguish routine responsibilities from the work that matters most in the first few months.

Separate essential and desirable requirements

Separate must-have requirements from nice-to-have preferences. Essential requirements should be genuinely necessary for the work, such as a legal licence, specific qualification or core technical skill. Desirable experience can help someone succeed, but it should not be presented as a condition when it is not required.

Realistic requirements make the role more accessible to capable candidates who may have gained equivalent experience through different routes. Avoid long wish lists that unnecessarily exclude people who could perform the work well.

Be transparent about the application process

Candidates should not have to search for the next step after reading a post. Explain how to apply and list the documents required. Say whether screening questions are included and describe the expected hiring stages, such as an initial review, assessment, interview or reference check.

Where possible, explain who will contact candidates and what communication timeline to expect. Even a short note about when applicants should hear back is more useful than leaving every candidate uncertain.

Show what candidates can expect

Practical details help candidates decide whether the opportunity is suitable before they invest time in an application. Include a salary or salary range where possible, along with relevant benefits, working hours, location and travel requirements.

Make the closing date visible and explain any accessibility or accommodation support available during the application or interview process. Candidates should be able to understand the working arrangement and the conditions of the opportunity without having to infer them.

Avoid misleading language

A trustworthy job post uses accurate language. Avoid inflated titles, unrealistic experience requirements and hidden unpaid work. Phrases such as “fast-paced” should have context, such as the size of the team, typical workload or changing priorities.

Check the post for discriminatory or exclusionary language. Do not promise progression, training or benefits that the employer cannot support. Honest detail creates better expectations for both the candidate and the hiring team.

Use a final recruiter checklist

Before publishing, read the post as someone who knows nothing about the role and check:

  • Is the title searchable and specific?
  • Are responsibilities clear?
  • Are essential requirements realistic?
  • Is the location and work arrangement clear?
  • Is the closing date visible?
  • Is the application route working?
  • Are screening questions relevant?
  • Is the communication process explained?
